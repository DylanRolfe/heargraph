# HearGraph video transcript

Descriptions of what is on screen and non-speech sounds are in [square brackets].

[The HearGraph home page.]

This is HearGraph, my solo entry for AccessED Hacks. I'm Dylan Rolfe.

[A worksheet graph of velocity against time, shown as a picture, with the question "At what time does the cart stop?"]

The barrier. A student who is blind or has low vision gets a physics worksheet. The graph is a picture. A screen reader just says: "graph of velocity, image." The student can't read a value or find where the line crosses zero without someone's help.

[The home page. The "I'm a teacher: make an accessible graph" button is chosen.]

The change. The teacher usually has the numbers behind the graph. In HearGraph, they paste them in.

[The teacher page. The form is filled with a title and a nine-row table of time and velocity. "Create link" is pressed and a link appears.]

A title, a small table, and Create link. The data is stored inside the link itself. No account, no upload, nothing saved on a server.

[The link is opened. The student page shows the title "Cart on a track: velocity vs time".]

The student opens the link.

[The Summary section: "Velocity starts at 6 m/s and ends at -6 m/s. It falls steadily the whole way. It reaches 0 m/s at 4 s and is negative after that."]

First, a short description in plain language: where the line starts, how it changes, and where it reaches zero.

[Keyboard focus moves to the graph, a straight line falling from top left to bottom right. Each press of the Right arrow moves a ring to the next point, and a short tone plays, lower each time.]

Then the graph itself. One Tab stop. The arrow keys move point by point, and every point is announced with its units.

[The Z key is pressed. The ring jumps to the point where the line meets the zero line. A tone and a click play.]

The question was: when does the cart stop? Press Z to jump to the zero crossing.

[Announcement:] "Time 4 seconds, velocity 0 metres per second, crossing zero."

[The Space key is pressed. Nine tones play, falling in pitch. Two high ticks at the first point, a click at the middle point, two low ticks at the last point.]

Space plays the whole graph as sound. Pitch follows the value. A click marks zero. Two low ticks mark the lowest value.

[The data table with columns "Time (s)" and "Velocity (m/s)". Then the practice questions. "4" is typed for the first question and the feedback reads "Correct. The cart stops when its velocity is 0 m/s."]

The same numbers are in a real data table. Then practice questions, with no timer, and feedback that explains the answer.

[The Settings page. High contrast and Large text are turned on. The student page now shows white and light orange on black with larger text.]

For low vision there's high contrast, large text, and a ringed, labelled focus point. After the first visit it works offline.

[The About page, Limitations list.]

What it doesn't do. The teacher has to supply the numbers. It can't read a picture. Sound is an extra, not a replacement for tactile graphics or a specialist teacher. And I tested it myself with automated checks and a screen reader. It has not been tested with blind or low-vision students.

[The README, AI assistance disclosure section.]

I built it during the event with an AI coding agent. The details and credits are in the README. Thanks for watching.
