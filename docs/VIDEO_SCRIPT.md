# HearGraph video script (about 2 minutes 40 seconds)

Record at 1280x720 or larger with a screen recorder that captures system audio (so the screen reader and the tones are heard). Speak slowly. The matching captions are in `docs/captions.srt` and the plain transcript is in `docs/TRANSCRIPT.md`. If your recording runs at a different pace, adjust the caption times before uploading.

If you record with a screen reader running, say so in the video ("this is NVDA with Firefox"). If you record without one, say that the text in the bold box under the graph is what is sent to the screen reader. Do not imply anything was tested with students.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Title card or landing page | "This is HearGraph, my solo entry for AccessED Hacks. I'm Dylan Rolfe." |
| 0:07 | A plain worksheet image of a velocity graph (or the printable page), with the question "At what time does the cart stop?" | "The barrier. A student who is blind or has low vision gets a physics worksheet. The graph is a picture. A screen reader just says: graph of velocity, image. The student can't read a value or find where the line crosses zero without someone's help." |
| 0:26 | Landing page, then click "I'm a teacher" | "The change. The teacher usually has the numbers behind the graph. In HearGraph, they paste them in." |
| 0:34 | Teacher page: click "Fill the form with example data", scroll the table, click "Create link" | "A title, a small table, and Create link. The data is stored inside the link itself. No account, no upload, nothing saved on a server." |
| 0:50 | Click "Open the student view" | "The student opens the link." |
| 0:54 | Student page: Summary section | "First, a short description in plain language: where the line starts, how it changes, and where it reaches zero." |
| 1:05 | Tab to the graph; press Right arrow three times | "Then the graph itself. One Tab stop. The arrow keys move point by point, and every point is announced with its units." (pause to let the announcements be heard) |
| 1:22 | Press Z | "The question was: when does the cart stop? Press Z to jump to the zero crossing." (pause) "Time 4 seconds, velocity 0 metres per second, crossing zero." |
| 1:35 | Press Space, let the sweep play | "Space plays the whole graph as sound. Pitch follows the value. A click marks zero. Two low ticks mark the lowest value." (stay quiet while it plays) |
| 1:50 | Scroll to the data table, then Practice. Type 4, press Enter | "The same numbers are in a real data table. Then practice questions, with no timer, and feedback that explains the answer." |
| 2:05 | Settings page: turn on High contrast and Large text; go back to the student page | "For low vision there's high contrast, large text, and a ringed, labelled focus point. After the first visit it works offline." |
| 2:18 | About page, Limitations list | "What it doesn't do. The teacher has to supply the numbers. It can't read a picture. Sound is an extra, not a replacement for tactile graphics or a specialist teacher. And I tested it myself with automated checks and a screen reader. It has not been tested with blind or low-vision students." |
| 2:38 | README, AI disclosure section | "I built it during the event with an AI coding agent. The details and credits are in the README. Thanks for watching." |
| 2:46 | End | |

## Recording tips

- Close other tabs and turn off notifications.
- Zoom the browser to 110 to 125 percent so text is readable in the video.
- Keep the mouse still while using the keyboard so viewers can see nothing is being clicked.
- Upload `captions.srt` with the video, and paste `TRANSCRIPT.md` into the video description.
